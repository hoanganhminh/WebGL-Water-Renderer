The Engineering Behind WebGL Water

In 2011, college student Evan Wallace (who later co-founded Figma and authored esbuild) revolutionized browser graphics by building this real-time water simulation using raw WebGL shaders.

🌊 1. Ping-Pong Heightfield Simulation

The water surface is modeled as a 2D discrete heightfield stored in two 32-bit floating-point textures (OES_texture_float). By ping-ponging between them, the shader solves the 2D wave equation: ∂²h/∂t² = c² ∇²h, tracking both height and velocity at 256×256 grid resolution.

✨ 2. Real-Time Caustics via Mesh Projection

Evan Wallace invented an ingenious technique for real-time caustics: a subdivided mesh representing the water surface is projected down along refracted sunlight rays onto the pool floor. By evaluating dFdx and dFdy screen-space derivatives, compressed triangles become bright highlights and stretched triangles become dim shadows.

📐 3. Raytraced Scene & Analytic Ambient Occlusion

Instead of standard rasterized geometry, the water surface shader raytraces reflections and refractions through Snell's law and Fresnel equations against the analytic pool cube bounding box and the floating sphere.

🪐 4. Interactive Buoyancy Physics

The sphere experiences gravitational acceleration, quadratic water drag, and submerged volume displacement, allowing dynamic splashing and tossing in full 3D space.
